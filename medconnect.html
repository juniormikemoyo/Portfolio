Mobile app · UI/UX case study

MedConnect — healthcare in your palm

A mobile app for finding a doctor, booking an appointment and consulting by video or voice call, designed end to end across 12 screens.

My roleUI/UX design
PlatformMobile app
Scope12 screens · 7 flows
FocusBooking · Consultation · Health tracking
01Overview

What is MedConnect?

MedConnect lets people find a doctor, see when they are available, book an appointment and talk to them from their phone.

I designed the whole product experience: onboarding and account creation, doctor discovery, booking, health tracking, voice and video consultation, and profile settings. The design is a single, consistent purple visual language across all 12 screens.

02The problem

Seeing a doctor is harder than it should be

Getting care often means phone tag, travelling to a clinic and waiting in a queue without knowing if the doctor is free. Health details are scattered, so patients and doctors start from zero each time.

03The goal

Make care simple to reach

Let someone go from “I need a doctor” to a confirmed appointment in a few taps, and from there to a conversation with that doctor, without leaving home. The app should feel calm and trustworthy because people use it when they are worried.

04The solution

Six things the app does well

Find the right doctorBrowse by specialty, see how many doctors are available and who the top doctors are.
Real-time availabilityPick a date and a time slot and book in a few taps, without phone calls or queues.
Video & voice consultationTalk to a doctor from home with simple call controls: video, message, speaker, mute and keypad.
Health at a glanceA heart-health view with the latest diagnosis and vitals such as heart rhythm and blood pressure.
Secure by designPrivate health data and sign-in with email, Google or Facebook.
Your account, your wayEdit your profile, choose language and location, manage notifications, history and downloads.
05User flow

From first launch to a doctor call

Intro→Sign up / Sign in→Onboarding→Find a doctor→Book a slot→Consult by call→Track health

Account tools (profile and settings) sit alongside the main path, reachable from the bottom bar at any time. The screens are shown in this order below.

06Design decisions

Why the screens look the way they do

01
Short, guided onboarding

A two-step flow with a progress bar and “Step 1 of 2” labels keeps sign-up short. The second step asks for basic details (age, height, weight, gender) so the app can personalise the experience.

Screens 04–05 ↓
02
Sign in the way people already do

Email and password sit next to Google and Facebook buttons, with a clear switch between “Sign in” and “Sign up”.

Screens 02–03 ↓
03
A home built around one question

The headline “Let’s find your suitable doctor” leads straight into specialty cards (with doctor counts and avatars) and a list of top doctors, each with a Book Appointment button. A five-icon bottom bar keeps Home, appointments, search, help and settings one tap away.

Screen 06 ↓
04
Booking that shows everything before you commit

The doctor’s profile shows experience, patients and reviews, with tabs for Schedules, About, Reviews and Experience. Date chips and Morning / Afternoon / Evening slots make time-picking quick, and one strong “Book Appointment” button sits at the bottom.

Screen 07 ↓
05
Health data made scannable

A heart-health screen puts the last diagnosis and a Diagnostic button first, then small cards for heart rhythm and blood pressure that you can swipe through.

Screen 08 ↓
06
A calm consultation screen

The call screen uses a soft lavender gradient, a large doctor photo, the call status and a 3 × 2 grid of controls, with a red end-call button that is easy to find.

Screens 09–10 ↓
07
Account control without clutter

Edit-profile fields are simple and spaced out. Settings are a plain list with chevrons, and the destructive actions (log out, delete account) are separated and coloured red.

Screens 11–12 ↓
07Visual design

One calm purple system

A deep indigo carries the brand and every primary action. Soft lavender tints separate cards and panels, and red is kept for the two moments that need attention: ending a call and deleting an account.

Rounded cards, pill-shaped buttons and chips keep the interface friendly, and a clean sans-serif with strong headings keeps it easy to scan. Large tap targets and generous spacing make key actions hard to miss.

08Next steps

What I would do next

Test the booking flow with real patients and doctors, check text contrast on the lavender surfaces, and design empty, loading and error states (for example, no slots available or a dropped call). After that, I would extend the health view beyond the heart to other vitals.
